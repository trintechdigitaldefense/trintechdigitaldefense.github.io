STAY SAFE.
STAY ALERT.

You can't control the threats out there. But you can control your defenses. These tips cover the fundamentals every person and business should follow.

Read the Guide

🔒 PASSWORDS & AUTHENTICATION

Weak passwords are the #1 entry point for attackers. Fix this first.

TIP 01

Use a Password Manager

Store unique passwords for every account. Tools like Bitwarden, KeePass, or 1Password generate and store 20+ character random passwords. You only need to remember one master password.

Essential
TIP 02

Enable 2FA Everywhere

Two-factor authentication adds a second verification step. Use an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Authy, Microsoft Authenticator) — never SMS if you can avoid it. SIM-swapping bypasses SMS 2FA entirely.

Essential
TIP 03

No Password Reuse

One breach exposes every account with the same password. Attackers run automated "credential stuffing" attacks against every breached database. Your bank password stolen from a gym site? They try it everywhere.

Essential
TIP 04

Never Share Verification Codes

No legitimate company will ever call, text, or email asking for your 2FA code, OTP, or password. If they do, they're the attacker. Block and report immediately.

Essential
TIP 05

Use Passkeys Where Available

Passkeys (biometric authentication) are the future of passwordless login. They resist phishing and can't be stolen via keyloggers. Enable on Google, Apple, Microsoft, and any service that supports them.

👁 PHISHING & SOCIAL ENGINEERING

The most common attack vector. It's not a tech problem — it's a human problem.

TIP 06

Hover Before You Click

Hover over every link in an email before clicking. Does the URL match the sender? Is it a legitimate domain or a lookalike? Example: `secure-bank-login.com` is not `bankoftrinidad.com`. Check the full domain, not just the visible text.

Essential
TIP 07

Verify Unexpected Messages

If you get an email or text that seems urgent — account suspended, package delivery, invoice due — don't act on it. Open a new browser tab, navigate to the official site directly, and log in there. The link in the message could lead anywhere.

Essential
TIP 08

Suspicious Attachments = Kill

Never open attachments from senders you didn't expect. Macros in Word documents, ZIP files, PDFs with embedded links — all common delivery methods for malware. If in doubt, delete or forward to your IT team.

Essential
TIP 09

Check for Urgency Tactics

Phishing relies on creating panic: "Your account will be deleted in 24 hours." "Unauthorized login detected — click here." Legitimate security alerts come through official channels, not urgent emails asking for immediate action.

Essential
TIP 10

Inspect Email Headers

Even senders that look legitimate can be spoofed. Check the "From" header, SPF/DKIM/DMARC results, and the actual return-path domain. Tools like Mail-Tester.com or Mimecast's email verifier can analyze suspicious messages.

📷 NETWORKS & WIRELESS

Your network is your digital front door. Guard it.

TIP 11

Avoid Public WiFi for Sensitive Work

Public networks (cafes, airports, hotels) let other users on the same network see your traffic. Even encrypted HTTPS is safer than HTTP, but attackers can perform SSL-stripping attacks. Use mobile data or a VPN for banking, work logins, and sensitive transactions on public WiFi.

Essential
TIP 12

Secure Your Home Router

Change the default admin password. Use WPA3 (or WPA2 at minimum). Disable WPS. Turn off remote management. Update firmware regularly. Your router is the gateway to every device in your home.

Essential
TIP 13

Create a Guest Network

Separate IoT devices (cameras, smart TVs, speakers) from your main network. These devices are poorly secured and commonly compromised, giving attackers a path into your personal devices.

🔌 DEVICE SECURITY

Every connected device is a potential attack surface.

TIP 14

Keep Everything Updated

OS updates, browser updates, app updates — they all contain security patches. Turn on automatic updates. Unpatched software is the easiest entry point for attackers. Most breaches exploit known vulnerabilities with patches already available.

Essential
TIP 15

Use Full-Disk Encryption

BitLocker (Windows), FileVault (Mac), device encryption (Android/iOS). If your laptop or phone is stolen, unencrypted data is accessible to anyone. Encryption requires the password to unlock.

Essential
TIP 16

Install Antivirus & Firewall

Windows Defender is sufficient for most users. macOS has built-in protection but isn't immune. Android apps from Google Play Store are generally safe. Never sideload APKs from unknown sources.

Essential
TIP 17

Lock Your Devices

Use a PIN, pattern, password, or biometric lock. Never use your device without screen lock protection. A simple PIN beats a thief having 30 seconds of access. Enable auto-lock after 1-2 minutes of inactivity.

Essential
TIP 18

Remove Unused Apps & Extensions

Browser extensions with excessive permissions can track your browsing, steal cookies, or inject malicious code. Android apps requesting unnecessary permissions (contacts, SMS, location) can leak data. Audit regularly.

Recommended

🔐 DATA PRIVACY & DIGITAL FOOTPRINT

Your data is being collected right now. Know what's out there.

TIP 19

Check Your Email Breach History

Go to https://haveibeenpwned.com and enter your email addresses. It shows every known data breach where your credentials were exposed. Change passwords for any compromised accounts immediately.

Essential
TIP 20

Limit What You Share Online

Personal information on social media feeds attackers: birth dates, pet names, childhood schools — these are common password recovery questions. Review privacy settings, restrict who can see your posts, and avoid sharing sensitive personal details publicly.

Essential
TIP 21

Backup Your Data (3-2-1 Rule)

Keep 3 copies of important data, on 2 different media types, with 1 offsite/offline. Ransomware encrypts everything attached to your network. An offline backup is your only recovery path if encrypted.

Essential
TIP 22

Be Careful with QR Codes

QR code phishing ("quishing") is rising. Attackers print malicious QR codes on restaurant menus, parking receipts, or posters. Scanning could redirect to a phishing site or download malware. Verify the destination before scanning.

Recommended

🏫 BUSINESS & EMPLOYEE SECURITY

If you run a business or employ people, these are non-negotiable.

TIP 23

Employee Security Training

82% of breaches involve a human element. Regular phishing simulations, security awareness training, and clear reporting procedures turn employees from the weakest link into your first line of defense.

Essential
TIP 24

Principle of Least Privilege

Every employee should only have access to the systems and data required for their role. When they leave, revoke immediately. When roles change, adjust access. Over-provisioned accounts are the #1 source of insider threats and lateral movement.

Essential
TIP 25

Incident Response Plan

When a breach happens, every second counts. Have a documented plan: who to call, what to preserve, how to contain, when to notify authorities. Practice it. Without a plan, panic causes evidence destruction and delayed response.

Recommended
TIP 26

Vendor & Third-Party Risk

Your weakest link might not be internal. Vendors with access to your systems, cloud accounts, or data are an attack vector. Vet them, monitor their access, and require them to follow minimum security standards.
