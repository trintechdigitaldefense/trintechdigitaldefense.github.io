Cybersecurity Compliance: What Trinidad Businesses Need to Know

Most Trinidad and Tobago business owners think of cybersecurity compliance as an IT problem. It is not. It is a business risk problem that IT supports. When regulators, payment processors, or enterprise clients require you to demonstrate security controls, the cost of non-compliance is not a fine. It is lost business.

Three frameworks dominate the compliance landscape for businesses in Trinidad and Tobago: data protection legislation, PCI-DSS for payment card security, and ISO 27001 for information security management systems. Each has different requirements, different audiences, and different consequences for non-compliance.

Trinidad and Tobago Data Protection Act

The Data Protection Act of Trinidad and Tobago establishes legal requirements for how businesses collect, process, store, and transfer personal data. It applies to any business that handles information about individuals — customer records, employee information, client details, transaction data.

Key requirements include:

  • Purpose limitation: collecting data only for specified, legitimate purposes and not using it for anything else
  • Data minimization: collecting only the data that is necessary for the stated purpose
  • Security safeguards: implementing appropriate technical and organizational measures to protect personal data
  • Accountability: maintaining records of processing activities and being able to demonstrate compliance
  • Data breach notification: reporting significant data breaches to the Information Commissioner within the required timeframe

Many businesses in Trinidad and Tobago are unaware that the Data Protection Act applies to them. If you have a customer database, employee records, or any personal information stored digitally, you are subject to the Act. The enforcement framework continues to mature, and regulatory scrutiny is increasing.

Key fact: Non-compliance with data protection regulations can result in penalties of up to TTD $500,000 and/or imprisonment for responsible officers. Beyond fines, non-compliance damages client trust and can result in loss of contracts with larger organizations that require compliance verification.

PCI-DSS: The Payment Card Standard

Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI-DSS) applies to any business that stores, processes, or transmits credit card data. This is broader than most businesses realize. Even a restaurant that accepts card payments through a point-of-sale system handles cardholder data. Even a professional service firm that stores client card details on file is in scope.

PCI-DSS requirements include:

  • Building and maintaining a secure network (firewall configuration, no default passwords)
  • Protecting cardholder data (encryption, masking, restricted access)
  • Implementing strong access controls (unique IDs, least privilege, physical access controls)
  • Regularly monitoring and testing networks (network scans, log reviews, vulnerability assessments)
  • Maintaining an information security policy

Businesses that fail PCI-DSS compliance face fines from payment processors, loss of the ability to accept card payments, and increased transaction fees. The financial impact of losing payment card acceptance often far exceeds the cost of compliance.

ISO 27001: The International Standard

ISO 27001 is the international standard for information security management systems. Unlike PCI-DSS, which is specific to payment cards, ISO 27001 applies to all information assets — physical, digital, intellectual, financial.

Achieving ISO 27001 certification requires establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an information security management system. It is a comprehensive framework that covers organization governance, asset management, human resource security, physical security, communication security, operations security, access control, cryptographic controls, system acquisition and development, supplier relationships, incident management, business continuity, and compliance.

For Trinidad and Tobago businesses, ISO 27001 certification signals to international clients and partners that your organization manages information security to internationally recognized standards. It is a competitive advantage in government tenders, international contracts, and partnerships with organizations that require compliance verification.
